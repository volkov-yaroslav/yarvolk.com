## Project overview

Forsikrings Info is an insurance lead-generation project built in **WordPress** with **Fluent Forms Pro**. The website itself is intentionally simple, because the real value of the project sits in the form workflow rather than in a content-heavy frontend.

The business model is straightforward: the client acts as a middleman between people looking for better insurance offers and the actual insurance companies that buy qualified leads. Traffic is driven through **Google Ads** and **Facebook Ads**, and the website's job is to capture detailed lead information in a way that stays usable for visitors while producing valuable data for the companies receiving those leads.

That meant the form could not be a basic contact form. It needed to qualify people, route them through the right questions, and collect a lot of information without the experience collapsing into friction.

![Forsikrings Info homepage hero and the lead form entry point.](/images/portfolio/forsikringsinfo.webp)

## Challenge

The challenge in this project was not building a visually complex website. It was designing a form flow that could handle many insurance scenarios while still feeling organized from the user's point of view.

At the top level, the form begins with **3 parent categories**: **Private**, **Housing association**, and **Company**. Each of those branches opens a different path. The Private branch alone includes **16 selectable insurance subcategories**, while Housing association includes **7**, and Company includes **14**.

Users are allowed to choose multiple insurance types, which means the form then has to reveal only the follow-up questions that make sense for those specific selections. In practice, this is a much harder problem than a standard lead form because the logic has to stay readable for both the visitor and the admin maintaining it later.

## Form architecture

The exported Fluent Forms configuration shows how large the final solution became. The form contains **132 actual fields**, **56 elements with active conditional logic**, and **19 step markers** guiding the user through the full journey. It also uses a wide mix of field types, including **42 dropdowns**, **32 numeric fields**, **36 text-based inputs**, file upload, address fields, and agreement checkboxes.

That scale was handled through a structured step-by-step flow rather than one overwhelming page. Users move from the initial insurance type selection into more relevant question groups, and the form only expands where it has to.

For example, different insurance choices trigger different follow-up paths for things like vehicles, buildings, travel, family, business assets, or housing-association coverage. That lets the form gather much richer lead data than a generic quote request, while still keeping the process navigable.

## Lead handling and tracking

The end result was not just a frontend form. It was a lead pipeline.

Submitted entries were connected to the client's CRM workflow, and the exported configuration also shows supporting output options such as **email notifications** and a **Google Sheets feed** setup. In other words, the form was designed as a flexible collection layer that could serve both immediate sales handling and broader reporting needs.

I also added **Facebook Pixel** tracking with a dedicated lead event that fires only after a successful submission. That gives the client cleaner conversion data for campaign evaluation and helps improve ad performance over time by tracking actual completed leads rather than simple page visits.

The form was first deployed on **forsikringsassistenten.no**, and later the same form logic was copied over to **stormforsikring.no** as well. That reuse matters because it shows the solution was not just built for one landing page. It was structured strongly enough to be redeployed in a second insurance context without rebuilding everything from scratch.

## Result

The final website is simple by design, but the lead system behind it is not. It gives the client a way to collect detailed insurance requests across multiple market segments, qualify those requests through layered logic, and pass them into follow-up workflows that are useful for real commercial lead handling.

For me, this project stands out because it is a good example of where the real product is not the website itself, but the conversion engine inside it. The strongest part of the work was turning a large, branching insurance inquiry into a form experience that still feels manageable for users and valuable for the businesses buying the leads.
