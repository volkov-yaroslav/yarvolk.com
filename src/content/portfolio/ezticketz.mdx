## Project overview

EZticketz is a US-based ticketing company that focuses on live events, from concerts to comedy shows and similar entertainment experiences. The business launched in Chicago in 2022 and has already covered more than 100 events while selling over 10,000 tickets.

Through the system, the business has sold tickets to shows connected to artists such as **Three 6 Mafia**, **G Herbo**, **Paul Wall**, **Randy Houser**, **Frankie J**, **Kameron Marlowe**, **Corey Kent**, and **Conner Smith**, among others.

This project was built for **WebmastersDesktop**. The client wanted a more polished storefront for browsing events, but without replacing the existing Shopify ticketing and checkout flow that was already working for the business.

## Challenge

The main challenge was creating a frontend that felt more like a proper event platform rather than a simple shop. Visitors needed a better way to discover shows, view event details, and understand what they were buying before being sent into checkout.

At the same time, the ticket-purchase flow needed to stay connected to Shopify, since that was still the core commerce engine behind the business.

## Solution

I built the storefront in **WordPress** using **Blocksy** and **Greenshift**, with **Events Manager** handling event listings and archive structure. To make each event page more useful, I also used **ACF** for custom fields such as the ticket purchase URL.

That setup created a simple and practical content workflow. Events are published on the WordPress side with their own layout, description, date, time, and location details. When tickets are ready, the Shopify product link is added to the event entry, so visitors can browse everything inside the website first and then continue to Shopify only at the point of purchase.

![Events archive page with published shows.](/images/portfolio/ezticketz-1.webp)

## Event pages

Each event page was designed to give users the information they usually look for before buying: event description, venue details, date and time, directions, and a map. The call to action is kept clear, with a direct **Purchase Tickets** handoff to the corresponding Shopify product.

This helped separate content presentation from checkout logic. WordPress became the storefront and event discovery layer, while Shopify remained the transaction layer.

![Single event page with detailed information and purchase flow.](/images/portfolio/ezticketz-2.webp)

## Result

The final website gave EZticketz a cleaner and more structured public-facing platform while preserving the existing Shopify setup behind the scenes. Instead of forcing users directly into product pages, the site now gives each event a proper presentation and a clearer path from discovery to purchase.

For this project, the value was not in rebuilding the whole system from scratch. It was in improving the experience around it and creating a more useful storefront for a growing live-events business.
